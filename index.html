Introduction

Following Australia's whale highways

Australia's coastline forms part of a much larger marine system linking feeding, migration and breeding environments across the Southern Hemisphere. Looking at whale movement through both space and time helps place individual sightings within a broader ecological story rather than treating them as isolated records.

06

Conclusion

A recovery story that is not yet finished

Whale migration around Australia is best understood as a connected ecological process rather than a collection of isolated sightings. Seasonal occurrence records reveal how different species use the coastline at different times, while long-term aerial monitoring adds a more reliable view of population and breeding change. Together, the two datasets show why conservation success should be assessed across both geography and time instead of through one headline population number. Continued monitoring remains essential because changes in migration timing, breeding distribution or population composition may become visible before they are obvious at the national scale.
